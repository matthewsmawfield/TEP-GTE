Appendix A: Mathematical Formalism

A.1 Full Action

The TEP action is formulated in the Einstein frame where gravity is canonical:

$$S = \int d^4x \sqrt{-g} \left[ \frac{M_{\text{Pl}}^2}{2} R - \frac{1}{2} K(\phi)\, g^{\mu\nu} \partial_\mu \phi\, \partial_\nu \phi - V(\phi) \right] + S_{\text{matter}}[\psi, \tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}, \phi]$$

where MPl is the reduced Planck mass, R is the Ricci scalar, K(φ) is the kinetic function, V(φ) is the screening potential, and Smatter is the matter action in the Jordan frame with coupling to the matter metric g̃μν.

A.2 Field Equations

Variation with respect to the gravitational metric yields the Einstein equations:

$$G_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{M_{\text{Pl}}^2} \left[ T_{\mu\nu}^{(\phi)} + T_{\mu\nu}^{(\text{matt})} \right]$$

where the scalar field stress-energy tensor is:

$$T_{\mu\nu}^{(\phi)} = K(\phi) \partial_\mu \phi \partial_\nu \phi - g_{\mu\nu} \left[ \frac{1}{2} K(\phi) g^{\rho\sigma} \partial_\rho \phi \partial_\sigma \phi + V(\phi) \right]$$

Variation with respect to the scalar field yields:

$$\nabla_\mu \left[ K(\phi) \nabla^\mu \phi \right] - \frac{\partial K}{\partial \phi} \frac{1}{2} g^{\mu\nu} \partial_\mu \phi \partial_\nu \phi - \frac{\partial V}{\partial \phi} = -\alpha(\phi) T + S_{\text{disf}}$$

where α(ϕ) ≡ d ln A/dϕ = βA/MPl is the conformal coupling strength (for A(ϕ) = exp(βAϕ/MPl)), T is the trace of the matter stress-energy tensor in the Jordan frame, and Sdisf contains disformal source terms proportional to B(ϕ).

A.3 Candidate Microscopic Completions and Nonlinear Screening

Illustrative Candidate Completion: This appendix presents an illustrative candidate completion (cubic Galileon derivative screening) that exhibits the (βAM/MPlΛ3)1/3 transition scale. In the canonical TEP architecture (Paper 0, Paper 4), environmental screening operates through continuous gradient flattening via the potential V(φ) and kinetic structure, independently of Horndeski derivative terms.

The following derivation illustrates one candidate microscopic completion (cubic Galileon) that realizes the continuous gradient suppression described as Temporal Shear in Section 4; it is not required for the empirical results.

One candidate completion invokes cubic Galileon terms in the action to generate nonlinear screening:

$$S_{\text{Galileon}} = \int d^4x \sqrt{-g} \left[\frac{c_3}{\Lambda^3} (\partial\phi)^2 \Box\phi\right]$$

where Λ is the strong-coupling scale (an energy scale, [M] in natural units c = ℏ = 1) and c3 is a dimensionless coefficient of order unity. With the canonical scalar field normalization (φ has mass dimension 1) and the TEP source term (βA/MPl)T, the nonlinear term becomes important when:

$$\frac{|\nabla \phi|^2}{\Lambda^3} \sim 1$$

The Vainshtein radius, inside which nonlinear effects dominate, is (in natural units c = ℏ = 1):

$$r_V = \left(\frac{\beta_A M}{M_{\text{Pl}} \Lambda^3}\right)^{1/3}$$

where βA is the conformal coupling, MPl = √(ℏc/G) is the Planck mass, and Λ is the strong-coupling energy scale. Restoring SI units:

$$r_V = \left(\frac{\beta_A M (\hbar c)^3}{M_{\text{Pl}} \Lambda^3}\right)^{1/3} = \left(\frac{\beta_A M}{M_{\text{Pl}}}\right)^{1/3} \frac{\hbar c}{\Lambda}$$

The factor βAM/MPl supplies the dimensionless source strength; (ℏc/Λ)3 supplies the length scale cubed. The bare expression (GM/Λ3)1/3 sometimes quoted in the Galileon literature is a shorthand that suppresses the βA/MPl coupling and implicitly assumes natural units with a specific field normalization; it is dimensionally incomplete as written.

For a spherically symmetric source of mass M, the scalar field profile in the Vainshtein regime (r < rV) is:

$$\phi(r) \sim \frac{\beta_A M_{\text{Pl}}}{M_{\text{Pl}}^2} \frac{GM}{r} \left(\frac{r}{r_V}\right)^3$$

This suppresses the Temporal Shear by a factor of (r/rV)3 inside the screening radius, reconciling weak-field cosmological behavior with strong-field solar system constraints. Other completions (e.g., chameleon mechanisms, symmetry-breaking potentials) may produce similar phenomenology.

A.4 Conservation Laws

The total stress-energy tensor is conserved:

$$\nabla_\mu \left[ T^{\mu\nu}_{(\phi)} + T^{\mu\nu}_{(\text{matt})} \right] = 0$$

However, the matter stress-energy alone is not conserved in the Einstein frame:

$$\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}_{(\text{matt})} = \alpha(\phi) T \nabla^\nu \phi + \text{disformal terms}$$

This apparent violation is resolved by transforming to the Jordan frame, where matter follows geodesics of g̃μν and energy-momentum is conserved with respect to the matter-frame covariant derivative.
