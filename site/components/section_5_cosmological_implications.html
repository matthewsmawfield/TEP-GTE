5. Consistency with Cosmological Phenomenology

Epistemic Status

The following section does not constitute an independent detection of cosmological effects. Instead, it addresses the question: if the terrestrial clock correlations reported in Sections 2–4 arise from a conformally coupled scalar field (whether screened via continuous gradient flattening or derivative completions), then what are the necessary consequences for astrophysical observations that already exist? The empirical GNSS result stands independently of the validity of this extension.

This section examines how the same scalar field producing λ ≈ 4,000 km correlations on Earth bears on gravitational lensing observables. The optical channels bounded in the tested Paper 19 configuration are scalar backreaction at κφ ∼ 10−5 for the SN Refsdal benchmark and the disformal transport term; the dynamical discriminant is the dynamical–lensing mass difference produced by active Temporal Shear. A source-independent CDM-free lensing potential remains a separate calculation. The framework yields testable expectations that may provide discriminators between particulate dark matter and conformal time-field interpretations at astrophysical scales.

Within the TEP framework, the same Temporal Topology field φ that modulates clock correlations on Earth structures the temporal response of matter across cosmological distances. The canonical TEP transition scale RT = (3M/4πρT)1/3, set by the environmental screening density ρT, connects terrestrial and astrophysical scales. The measured GNSS correlation-length family (azimuth-averaged λ = 3,210 ± 389 km; cross-sector dispersion λT = 4,201 ± 1,967 km) is interpreted as a characteristic empirical scale of the clock-sector covariance $C_A$ in Earth's environment, compared with the Earth-calibrated $R_T$ scale (Paper 0 §7). At galactic scales, the distinct shear response can be active in low-acceleration environments where SΣ ≃ 0.98. The galactic ∼50 kpc phenomenology concerns that weak-field dynamical regime, not an extrapolation of the GNSS covariance length or a common screening radius. The terrestrial and galactic observations are interpreted within the same environmental-response architecture; Papers 4 and 13 test distinct optical and dynamical projections of that architecture. Paper 4 identifies the dynamical–lensing mass difference as the phantom-mass consistency test; Paper 13 reports a SPARC-anchored wide-binary transition at Rs ≃ 2,646 ± 182 AU. Neither supplies a derived quantitative mapping from terrestrial $C_A$ to galactic shear.

5.1 GW170817 Constraints

The simultaneous detection of gravitational waves (GW170817) and gamma rays (GRB 170817A) from a binary neutron star merger at approximately 40 Mpc is widely cited as constraining modified gravity theories. The signals arrived within Δt = 1.74 ± 0.05 seconds after traveling some 130 million years, yielding:

$$\frac{|c_{\gamma} - c_g|}{c} \lesssim 10^{-15}$$

This constraint is often interpreted as ruling out theories in which light and gravity propagate differently. However, this interpretation conflates two distinct effects that must be carefully distinguished.

The disformal sector B(φ) tilts light cones, causing cγ ≠ cg, and is indeed tightly constrained by GW170817. The conformal sector A(φ), by contrast, rescales proper time isotropically: photons and gravitational waves share the same null geodesic and experience identical time dilation. Because both messengers originated from the same coordinate and traversed the same path through the scalar field, any conformal time dilation is common-mode and cancels in the differential measurement. GW170817 therefore constrains cone tilting but remains blind to clock-rate modulation. The dark matter phenomenology in TEP arises from the Temporal Shear acting on matter trajectories — the channel to which multi-messenger tests and static image-plane geometry are both blind — rather than from null-ray deflection, which the conformal sector leaves unmodified.

This can be seen explicitly in the conformal metric limit (B = 0):

$$ds^2 = A^2(\phi) g_{\mu\nu} dx^\mu dx^\nu = 0$$

Since A(φ) is an overall prefactor, the null condition ds² = 0 is identical for both the matter metric g̃μν (governing photons) and the gravitational metric gμν (governing gravitational waves). Their null cones coincide exactly: cγ = cg = c locally. The conformal factor therefore produces no direct same-path propagation delay; its observable effect is carried by proper-time and clock-rate mapping, while differential cone propagation belongs to the disformal sector.

5.2 The Isochrony Axiom and Observational Degeneracy

Standard gravitational lensing analysis assumes that all photons in a lensed image represent a synchronous snapshot of the source. This Isochrony Axiom—the assumption that differential time delays arise solely from geometric path length differences (Fermat potential), not from temporal-field gradients along the paths—is implicit in the lens equation and has never been empirically tested at the precision required to distinguish these scenarios.

Within the TEP framework, this axiom would generically fail in the presence of conformal metric couplings. Photons themselves accumulate no proper time (dτγ = 0) and conformal factors produce no additional same-path propagation delay; what varies is the matter-clock rate. Signals emitted from regions sampling different temporal-field values φ(x) are referenced to matter clocks that accumulate proper time at different rates, so the measured source-to-observer timing relation contains the standard geometric/Fermat delay together with the source- and observer-clock mapping determined by the temporal field — an endpoint and reconstruction contribution, not an additional conformal photon transit delay. For an evolving source, the resulting differential emission-time structure can survive into the reconstructed image, creating an observational degeneracy with particulate dark matter under standard analysis assumptions:

Consider two interpretations of the same Fermat potential surface. Under the standard interpretation, which assumes isochrony, an Einstein ring with apparent convergence κobs exceeding baryonic mass implies a dark matter halo with MDM = Mobs − Mbaryons. Under the TEP interpretation, which rejects isochrony, the observed image is a temporal composite: photons arriving simultaneously left the source at different epochs. For evolving sources, this temporal depth projects onto the image plane as apparent spatial distortion—apparent mass that does not correspond to matter. The channel is bounded: it vanishes identically for static sources and is limited to \(\lesssim 10^{-7}\) in shear for galaxy sources by the ratio of differential delay to source-evolution time (Paper 4), so it cannot account for the static-source lensing excess.

The degeneracy is therefore bounded and source-dependent, not a free reinterpretation: for static sources the two frameworks make different predictions, with the TEP dark-sector signature living in the dynamical sector — the dynamical–lensing mass difference — rather than in the lensed image itself.

5.3 Phantom Mass

Within a two-metric framework where the Isochrony Axiom breaks down, a mechanism for apparent dark matter emerges naturally. Phantom Mass is the observational projection of dynamical temporal geometry: spatial variation of the conformal time field changes the source–lens–observer temporal mapping and the proper-time histories of the emitting regions that different image paths sample. Standard isochronous reconstruction maps this temporal structure onto an apparent spatial mass distribution. Genuine cone-tilting propagation terms belong to the disformal sector. Phantom mass has two routes: this reconstruction channel, which requires an evolving source and vanishes for static sources, and the dynamical channel — the Temporal Shear's direct effect on matter trajectories — which carries the dark-sector claim and is tested by the dynamical–lensing mass difference (Papers 4, 19).

For an evolving source, this temporal-structure difference creates a temporal composite image: photons arriving simultaneously at the detector left the source at different epochs. Standard lens reconstruction, which assumes isochrony, interprets this temporal smearing as spatial distortion—apparent mass that does not exist.

The amplification matrix in TEP decomposes as:

$$\mathcal{A}_{ij} = \underbrace{\mathcal{A}^{\text{GR}}_{ij}}_{\text{Baryonic}} + \underbrace{\mathcal{A}^{(g[\phi],\text{static})}_{ij}}_{\text{bounded optical}} + \underbrace{\mathcal{A}^{(\text{dyn})}_{ij}}_{\text{Shear Noise}}$$

Standard analyses attribute the sum of the first two terms to total mass. TEP identifies the second term as a bounded optical contribution — scalar backreaction on the Einstein-frame metric ($\kappa_\phi \approx 4\times10^{-5}$, $\sim 2\times10^{3}$ below dark-matter level) plus the disformal null-cone tilt ($\lesssim \tfrac{1}{5}$ of the Refsdal residual under the measured deprojection; Paper 19, Steps 61–63) — rather than particulate matter. Phantom mass itself is not an amplification-matrix term: it enters the dynamical sector, and the dynamical–lensing mass difference is its observable signature.

5.3.1 Static versus Dynamic Time Delays

Observed strong-lens time delays (day-to-month scale, e.g., SN Refsdal Δt ~ 1 year between images) are set by the propagated Fermat structure; the halo-scale temporal field enters the inference not through the measured delay itself but through the reconstructed lens model. The distinction resolves through two complementary mechanisms.

Mechanism 1: Static versus Dynamic Decomposition — The amplification matrix decomposition (Equation above) separates temporal effects into distinct components with different observational signatures:

  • Static Temporal Structure (𝒜(g[φ],static)): The scalar's gravitational backreaction on gμν contributes real, structured curvature on halo scales — bounded at κφ ∼ 10−5, about 2×103 below dark-matter level (Paper 19, Step 61) — a smooth field on the image plane whose Hessian an isochronous reconstruction absorbs into the inferred convergence κ, with the mean level entering the mass-sheet normalization. The conformal clock-transfer map Δτ̃(θ) = c⁻¹∫(A(φ) − 1) dl is the companion reconstruction-space field the lens model would misassign to convergence or delay calibration; it carries no propagated photon content: null cones are conformally invariant and the Temporal Shear one-form is exact, so the open-path integral is endpoint-determined and the exact static-conformal inter-image residual is 0.0 d (Paper 19, step_56).
  • Source-evolution response (𝒜(dyn)): The gradient of the delay field ∇(Δτ̃) creates differential emission-time structure that depends on the ray's impact parameter and the source's evolutionary state. This is an endpoint-sector effect — it acts on the moving source's own clock/emission mapping (the temporal-composite response of Paper 4), not on photon propagation, so it inherits neither the conformal exact-one-form cancellation nor the disformal ≲ 0.2 s bound. For transient sources with variability timescale tvar, the dynamic contribution scales as Δτdynamic ~ ∇(Δτ̃) × vsource × tvar, where vsource is the source's proper motion or internal velocity field.

Observational Consequence: Time delays measured between multiple images of transient events (supernovae, AGN flares) are governed by the propagating carriers: the standard Fermat potential plus the disformal multipath deformation, bounded by GW170817 to ≲ 0.2 s per 2 Mpc halo path — far below any measured lens delay. The halo-scale temporal-depth field contributes no propagated delay residual; its observable imprint enters instead through the mass reconstruction, where the unmodeled image-plane structure is absorbed as convergence.

Mechanism 2: Screening Locality — The Temporal Topology field φ varies significantly only within the region where Temporal Shear is non-negligible, bounded roughly by the TEP transition scale RT ~ 100 kpc around the lens galaxy, where the environmental screening operator SΣ(ℰ) transitions from the suppressed to the unscreened regime. Beyond this radius, Temporal Topology flattens to its cosmological value, φ → φ0, and temporal-structure signatures cease accumulating. The integration path for the temporal-depth scale is therefore limited to the halo depth Lhalo ~ 2 Mpc (the physical extent of the dark matter halo), not the full cosmological distance DL ~ Gpc to the source. This locality ensures that the static temporal-depth scale remains far smaller than the cosmological light-travel time DL/c ~ 109 years.

$$\Delta \tau_{\text{depth}} \sim \frac{\epsilon}{2} \frac{L_{\text{halo}}}{c} \quad \text{(not } \frac{D_L}{c} \sim 10^9\text{ years)}$$

Testable Expectation for Time-Delay Cosmography: TEP implies a systematic offset in H0 measurements from time-delay cosmography, but through the mass-inference channel, not the image plane. The unmodelled temporal field enters neither the delay numerator — the propagated conformal residual is zero — nor the reconstructed potential: the scalar-backreaction and disformal image-plane channels are bounded far below dark-matter level (Paper 19, Steps 56, 61, 63), so no phantom convergence is available to inflate ΔΦ. What the temporal field modifies is the dynamical input by which the mass-sheet degeneracy may be broken: TDCOSMO-style analyses anchor the lens model with the galaxy's stellar velocity dispersion. Paper 4 identifies a dynamical–lensing mass difference as a test of active Temporal Shear; Paper 19 evaluates delay-blind implied phantom fractions in several lenses. Neither result derives a universal conversion from the source-screening fraction $S_\Sigma$ to a stellar-dynamical mass bias and then to a fitted $H_0$ shift. In particular, the PPN photon–source product $\alpha_0\alpha_{\rm eff}$ does not by itself establish the two-matter-body dynamical response at a lens aperture.

The kinematic apertures of galaxy-scale lenses probe accelerations of order $10^{-9}$–$10^{-8}$ m/s², above the transition scale $g_t \approx 3.4\times10^{-10}$ m/s². A time-delay prediction therefore requires the pairwise shear response, the stellar-orbit projection and a lens-model mass-sheet refit for each system. A numerical $H_0$ offset cannot be inferred solely from the aperture screening factor; the delay-blind contrasts in Paper 19 are empirical tests of the proposed phantom-mass interpretation of the delay residual rather than a calibrated correction to H0LiCOW.

Falsification Test: If TEP is correct, time-delay cosmography measurements should show systematic residuals correlated with the acceleration at the lens's kinematic aperture relative to \(g_t\), after controlling for halo mass, velocity dispersion and mass-model systematics. Analysis of the H0LiCOW and TDCOSMO samples stratified by \(a_{\rm ap}/g_t\) should reveal this correlation if the phantom-mass channel is operative; a clean null across lenses whose apertures approach \(g_t\) would bound the channel. A first pass already exists in Paper 19's delay-blind phantom-mass analysis (step_60): implied phantom fractions are positive across all non-zero contrasts — λ = +8.7 ± 2.6% (Refsdal SX:S1), +9.1 ± 5.8% (Encore), +9.5 ± 4.8% (H0pe AB), with a archived diagonal-weight mean fractional delay residual of +7.8 ± 2.0% (χ² = 2.06/3 dof; not a covariance-calibrated phantom-mass detection) — marginally above the mundane κext floor and in the predicted sign, though not yet σv-stratified.

5.4 The Variability-Mass Correlation

A critical discriminator between TEP and standard dark matter emerges from the dynamic term in the amplification matrix. Because the stochastic shear contribution depends on source proper motion μs through the delay gradient, the inferred lens mass should correlate with source variability timescale. Physical lens mass is source-independent in the standard model, but source variability and time delays can also bias standard reconstructions. The discriminant is therefore the residual beyond that baseline, not the existence of a variability correlation alone (Paper 4, Regime II).

Specifically, lenses observed through rapidly variable sources (AGN, quasars) should show systematically different mass reconstructions than the same lenses observed through slowly evolving sources (elliptical galaxies). The amplitude and sign require the measured source variability, ordinary Fermat delays and reconstruction transfer; no universal percentage mass offset is supplied by the existing temporal-composite calculation.

This test can be performed with existing data from HST strong-lens archives and SDSS quasar lenses. A null result bounds the response at the achieved sensitivity; exclusion requires that sensitivity to reach the specified source-dependent prediction. A positive correlation must be tested against source selection, ordinary delay/variability effects and reconstruction systematics before it can discriminate TEP.

5.5 Cross-Scale Dynamics: Saturation vs. Kinetic Transitions

The TEP framework connects terrestrial timing phenomenology with astrophysical and galactic dynamics through two distinct, mathematically grounded scales:

  • The Clock Sector (Geometric Saturation Scale RT): For macroscopic bodies of saturation density ρT ≈ 20 g/cm3, the geometric saturation radius follows RT(M) = (3M/4πρT)1/3 ∝ M1/3. Calibrated through Earth's GNSS clock-covariance correlation length (RT(M⊕) ≈ 4,150 km; Paper 6), this scale fixes the unsaturated clock-amplitude factor SA = min[1, (ρ̄/ρT)1/3] ≈ 0.65 for Earth. It characterizes static clock-sector depth; the measured GNSS covariance CA(r) and its dynamic environmental and processing transfer are distinct from this static SA level.
  • The Force / Shear Sector (Kinetic Transition Scale r*): For geodesic kinematics, orbital dynamics, and rotation curves, the master kinetic action P(X,φ) = X − V + X|X|/ΛX4 derives the two-body transition scale directly from first principles:
    $$r_*(M) = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{g_t}} \propto M^{1/2}$$
    where gt = cH₀/(2|βA|) ≈ 3.4 × 10−10 m/s2 with ΛX = √(MPl H₀) ≃ 1.9 meV (Paper 0 §2.2). In high-acceleration regimes (g ≫ gt), the kinetic operator suppresses the observable Temporal Shear to 𝒮Σ ≪ 10−5 (satisfying Cassini and Solar-System ephemeris bounds), while in low-acceleration regimes (g ≪ gt) the shear recovers unsuppressed (𝒮Σ → 1).

Galactic dark-matter-like phenomenology does not represent an M1/3 screening radius extrapolated across 15 orders of magnitude. Active Temporal Shear supplies extra matter acceleration, with a threefold unscreened dynamical enhancement under the unit-term branch and mass-inference assumptions of Paper 0 §7. Flat outer rotation curves and baryonic Tully–Fisher scaling are instead obtained in the alternative low-gradient realization already derived and evaluated against SPARC (Paper 6, Steps 10–13). The field-wide-binary transition Rs ≈ 2,646 AU (Paper 13) tests the pairwise recovery profile; it is not itself a derivation of the galactic outer force law. These results retain their branch and calibration provenance within the common matter-coupling architecture; the two kinetic realizations have not yet been shown to be a single action across local, binary and galactic regimes (Paper 0 §7). 5.6 Environmental Cepheid Response and the Hubble Tension

Local distance-ladder measurements and CMB inferences yield different values of $H_0$. Paper 11 tests whether an environment-dependent Cepheid response contributes to this discrepancy; its corrected distance-ladder value is conditional on the fitted response and the observing-chain model, not a direct clock-period prediction.

5.6.1 Cepheids as Environmental Clocks

Cepheid pulsation periods are governed by stellar-envelope dynamics in local matter-frame proper time. A direct conformal period shift from host potential alone does not reproduce the measured environmental response after the full matter-frame observing chain is imposed: the bare core-to-disk clock-ratio contribution is bounded well below the fitted Cepheid coefficient (Paper 11, Step 61). Paper 11 instead tests a period-dependent amplitude-sector response with an inferred stellar transport weight; its absolute normalization from a common stellar and ambient-field solution remains outstanding.

5.6.2 Empirical Detection of the Bias

Analysis of the SH0ES Cepheid sample (Paper 11), stratified by host-galaxy potential depth uφ = Vrot/√2 (a TEP-independent kinematic proxy reconstructed from pinned HyperLEDA rotation velocities), reveals a positive correlation between potential depth and derived H₀ — an environmental ordering tested by Paper 11's revised amplitude-sector response, not a detection of the bare clock-ratio mechanism. Split at the sample median (uφ = 114.6 km/s), the deep-potential subsample yields H₀ = 68.49 ± 3.21 km/s/Mpc against 66.99 ± 2.50 km/s/Mpc for the shallow-potential subsample (full host-distance covariance propagated), and the continuous endpoint response ΓX = (1.156 ± 0.958) × 10⁷ km/s/Mpc (1.2σ at the canonical σv = 250 km/s convention; 2.0σ at the σv = 150 km/s sensitivity variant) returns a positive sign in all 37 leave-one-host-out refits. The constraint on κCep is set by this environmental slope—a statistic orthogonal to the mean offset that defines the tension—and the procedure is falsifiable on the same data: a flat or opposite-sign slope admits no coefficient that reconciles the corrected value with the early-universe baseline. Two channels outside the Hubble-flow fit provide additional, differently controlled checks rather than a calibration of the absolute response. The Cepheid–TRGB differential on the matched hosts—regressing μCep − μTRGB against the same environmental coordinate, independent of redshift information—returns κCep = (0.32 ± 0.39) × 10⁶ mag, a consistent point estimate at presently low significance; and the internal radial P–L gradient within the LMC (+0.0284 ± 0.0086 mag, 3.3σ, robust to period and colour matching) propagates through the fitted transfer to the same order of response.

Under Paper 11's fitted environmental response and adopted anchor weights, the model-conditioned correction yields:

$$H_0 = 66.65 \pm 1.58 \text{ km/s/Mpc}$$

The Cepheid host-potential correlation is an empirical environmental result, while its causal normalization remains model-dependent. Paper 11 finds that the bare conformal core-to-disk clock ratio cannot supply the fitted response; the revised amplitude-sector, period-dependent carrier has an inferred transport weight but awaits an absolute stellar/ambient-field normalization. Its corrected local $H_0 = 66.65 \pm 1.58$ km/s/Mpc agrees with the companion TEP-CMB value $66.70 \pm 0.58$ km/s/Mpc at 0.03σ within the framework, not as an independent confirmation of the mechanism. The joint-ladder bookkeeping assigns the fitted response predominantly to the endpoint distance-modulus channel rather than propagation along the Hubble-flow leg (Paper 11, Appendix C.4). The environmental host-level slope is modest at the canonical velocity-scatter convention, and host-uniform shifts can be absorbed into latent distance moduli; the within-host period-coupled term provides a more identifiable test. The corrected value is therefore a conditional inference, not an established resolution of the Hubble tension.

5.8 Connection to Cosmological Tensions

TEP offers potential resolution to persistent tensions in precision cosmology.

Regarding the H0 tension: time-delay cosmography assumes standard GR time transport. If TEP's temporal structure contributes to lens delays, the inferred H0 would be biased, potentially explaining why independent time-delay lensing measurements falsely agree with the uncorrected distance ladder, rather than tracking the true CMB value.

Regarding the S8 tension: galaxy sources can carry additional source-dependent reconstruction covariance absent from the CMB source-evolution channel. Paper 4 bounds this temporal-composite contribution far below the percent-level mean-amplitude discrepancy; it does not explain the observed S8 offset. The TEP-HC weak-lensing comparison is a live tension under a CDM-containing likelihood, not a CDM-free resolution. A CDM-free prediction of the source-independent CMB and galaxy lensing potentials remains required.

The source-class dependence is a bounded prediction to test against ordinary reconstruction and selection effects; it is not a demonstrated explanation of the mean S8 discrepancy or CMB deflection.
