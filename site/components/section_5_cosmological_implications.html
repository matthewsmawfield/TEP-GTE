5.1 GW170817 Constraints

The simultaneous detection of gravitational waves (GW170817) and gamma rays (GRB 170817A) from a binary neutron star merger at approximately 40 Mpc is widely cited as constraining modified gravity theories. The signals arrived within Δt = 1.74 ± 0.05 seconds after traveling some 130 million years, yielding:

$$\frac{|c_{\gamma} - c_g|}{c} \lesssim 10^{-15}$$

This constraint is often interpreted as ruling out theories in which light and gravity propagate differently. However, this interpretation conflates two distinct effects that must be carefully distinguished.

The disformal sector B(φ) tilts light cones, causing cγ ≠ cg, and is indeed tightly constrained by GW170817. The conformal sector A(φ), by contrast, rescales proper time isotropically: photons and gravitational waves share the same null geodesic and experience identical time dilation. Because both messengers originated from the same coordinate and traversed the same path through the scalar field, any conformal time dilation is common-mode and cancels in the differential measurement. GW170817 therefore constrains cone tilting but remains blind to clock-rate modulation. The dark matter phenomenology in TEP arises from the conformal sector acting on measured lapse and arrival-time structure — the channel to which multi-messenger tests and static image-plane geometry are both blind — rather than from null-ray deflection, which the conformal sector leaves unmodified.

This can be seen explicitly in the conformal metric limit (B = 0):

$$ds^2 = A^2(\phi) g_{\mu\nu} dx^\mu dx^\nu = 0$$

Since A(φ) is an overall prefactor, the null condition ds² = 0 is identical for both the matter metric g̃μν (governing photons) and the gravitational metric gμν (governing gravitational waves). Their null cones coincide exactly: cγ = cg = c locally. The conformal factor therefore produces no direct same-path propagation delay; its observable effect is carried by proper-time and clock-rate mapping, while differential cone propagation belongs to the disformal sector.

5.3.1 Static versus Dynamic Time Delays

Observed strong-lens time delays (day-to-month scale, e.g., SN Refsdal Δt ~ 1 year between images) probe only the differential dynamic component of the temporal field, not the integrated temporal-depth scale accumulated across the halo. This component is source-independent and manifests as an overall convergence κ that standard lens reconstruction attributes to total mass. Because it is common to all images, it does not contribute to differential time delays between multiple images.
  • Dynamic Shutter (𝒜(α,dyn)): The gradient of the delay field ∇(Δτ) creates differential delays that depend on the ray's impact parameter and the source's evolutionary state. For transient sources with variability timescale tvar, the dynamic contribution scales as Δτdynamic ~ ∇(Δτ) × vsource × tvar, where vsource is the source's proper motion or internal velocity field.

Observational Consequence: Time delays measured between multiple images of transient events (supernovae, AGN flares) probe only the differential dynamic component, not the integrated temporal-depth scale. Day-to-month delays reflect the gradient-driven differential component, while the static temporal-structure scale affects convergence measurements.

$$\Delta \tau_{\text{depth}} \sim \frac{\epsilon}{2} \frac{L_{\text{halo}}}{c} \quad \text{(not } \frac{D_L}{c} \sim 10^9\text{ years)}$$

5.4 The Variability-Mass Correlation

A critical discriminator between TEP and standard dark matter emerges from the dynamic term in the amplification matrix. If such a correlation exists, it constitutes positive evidence for TEP independent of GNSS timing or FRB observations.

5.6 Resolving the Hubble Tension: The Cepheid Bias

The Hubble Tension—the persistent 5σ discrepancy between local distance-ladder measurements (H₀ ≈ 73 km/s/Mpc) and early-universe CMB inference (H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc)—finds a natural resolution in the TEP framework through the violation of the Isochrony Axiom in stellar environments.

5.6.1 Cepheids as Environmental Clocks

Cepheid variable stars function as "standard clocks" whose pulsation periods P are governed by the internal physics of their stellar envelopes. Standard cosmology assumes that these periods are invariant for stars of a given mass and luminosity. TEP predicts that these periods are modulated by the local gravitational potential Φ through the conformal factor A(φ). In deep potentials (high velocity dispersion σ), the scalar field induces period contraction:

$$P_{\text{obs}} = P_{\text{true}} (1 - \eta |\Phi|/c^2)$$

When observers apply a universal Period-Luminosity (P-L) relation, this period contraction is misinterpreted as diminished intrinsic luminosity. This leads to systematically underestimated distances to host galaxies with deep potential wells, which in turn inflates the derived local Hubble constant.
