7. Conclusions

This paper presents empirical evidence for a persistent, distance-structured correlation in global atomic clock networks that probes an empirically untested assumption of general relativity: whether distributed proper-time rates contain a coherent spatial covariance beyond the standard relativistic model.

7.1 Summary of Findings

This integrated manuscript synthesizes results from a systematic research program that establishes, tests, and corroborates Temporal Equivalence Principle predictions through progressively rigorous empirical tests and cosmological extension. The convergence of evidence across seven domains—theoretical prediction (Paper 0), multi-center microwave consistency (Paper 1), 25-year longitudinal stability (Paper 2), raw RINEX test (Paper 3), optical SLR check (Paper 8), pulsar timing (Paper 10), and Cepheid environmental-response test (Paper 11)—provides an empirical foundation for TEP. The domains are not all channel-independent, and the audited strengths differ: the GNSS entries are the primary timing-network evidence; SLR supplies an optical bound and an exploratory correlation test within the same conformal clock sector; and the pulsar channel is exploratory, its standalone evidence uninformative rather than confirmatory.

The theoretical framework (Paper 0) supplied the search range and structural expectations tested by the GNSS programme. Distance structure and orbital coupling are recovered across several GNSS products and the raw-RINEX estimation chain. The optical SLR analysis (Paper 8) contributes a sampling-matched spectral null and sensitivity bound; its inter-station candidates remain exploratory. Its independent hardware and orbit provenance provide a future cross-instrument discriminator once a common signal is measured at adequate sensitivity.

Paper 11 tests an environment-dependent Cepheid response and reports a conditional corrected value H₀ = 66.65 ± 1.58 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹. Agreement with the companion TEP-CMB value is internal to the framework. The canonical joint host-level response is about 1.1σ, while the within-host period term is 2.57σ in the joint fit with host-assignment permutation p = 0.0125. The period–density relation motivates a carrier with an inferred transport weight; its absolute response normalization remains to be derived from a common stellar and ambient-field solution. These empirical tests constrain that physical completion.

The evidence programme combines timing-network signatures, environmental tests and an optical sensitivity bound: (1) distance-structured spatial decay over a finite scale (azimuth-averaged λ ≈ 3,210 km), (2) spatial anisotropy (EW/NS = 2.16), (3) orbital velocity coupling (r = −0.888, 3.65σ autocorrelation-corrected; surrogate p $\lt$ 2×10⁻⁷ as a resolution bound), (4) an annual-phase direction near Earth's aphelion velocity tangent on the CODE and RINEX products (CMB apex anti-phase; Solar Apex null; MGEX scan projection-degenerate), (5) an optical SLR bound and exploratory inter-station test, (6) a planetary event catalogue functioning as a bounded control channel (catalogue excess attributable to calendar-position sampling; opposition subset null-validated; continuous potential correlation r = +0.116, p = 0.012), (7) 18.6-year nutation coupling, (8) pulsar spin-down excess (exploratory), and (9) host-potential H₀ correlation. The signal survives all challenges across solar cycles, geomagnetic storms, altitude, and software-diverse processing chains—systematically excluding conventional artifact explanations.

7.5 Concluding Remarks

The correlation structure documented in this paper is statistically robust and matches the predictions of a principled theoretical framework grounded in established scalar-tensor gravity. Whether it ultimately reflects new physics or reveals subtle systematics yet to be identified, the signal demands explanation.

For three decades, standard GNSS processing systematically removed a ~4,000 km correlation as part of routine noise filtering. The algorithms were designed to optimize positioning accuracy rather than to characterize fundamental physics. The correlation structure reported here was present in the residuals but categorized as systematic error rather than physical phenomenon.

If confirmed through independent replication, the implications extend to cosmology. The Isochrony Axiom—the assumption that all photons in a lensed image represent a synchronous snapshot—has never been empirically tested. If this axiom fails in the presence of conformal metric couplings, apparent dark matter arises in the dynamical sector as a geometric artifact: the Temporal Shear's effect on matter trajectories projected onto a Newtonian mass model. The artifact does not extend to photon paths — null curves are conformally invariant — so its spatial-sector signature is the dynamical–lensing mass difference — the phantom mass appearing only in dynamics — rather than a second apparent mass in lensing (Paper 19).

Einstein established that simultaneity is relative. The TEP framework suggests that simultaneity may be generally non-integrable—that the rate of proper time accumulation is itself a dynamical field. The speed of light remains exactly invariant locally, but global synchronization becomes path-dependent.

The ~4,000 km correlation on Earth (cross-sector mean and directional dispersion λT = 4,201 ± 1,967 km — an order-of-magnitude measurement) and the ~50 kpc dark-matter-attributed dynamical phenomenology in galaxies are interpreted within TEP as manifestations of the same environment-dependent Temporal-Topology response, pending independent replication, completion-dependent transfer calculations, and lensing–dynamics consistency (Paper 19), connected by the continuous relaxation of Temporal Topology from deep potential wells to the weak-field regime. The M1/3 scaling appearing in some candidate completions remains testable through interplanetary missions and pulsar timing arrays, but it is not the primary empirical claim. This hypothesis is falsifiable through the experimental program outlined in Section 6. Independent replication is essential, and the data and analysis code are publicly available. The community is invited to verify, challenge, or refute these findings.
